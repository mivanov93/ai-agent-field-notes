# The agreement ladder

*The tested artifact, verbatim, from the design project where the
incidents in [settled is a human word](../settled-is-a-human-word.md)
happened. The owner wrote it, revised it after adversarial review, runs
it in that project's instruction file, and reports it holding. Read it
in that note's terms: the ladder is vigilance support, not prevention —
it makes rounding-up cheap to catch (rungs are greppable, quotes are
checkable) and the catching stays the owner's job.*

Why it exists: when reviews or critics produce findings in bulk, each
handoff quietly upgrades the level of agreement — a reviewer's claim
becomes a problem "we have," an acknowledged problem becomes a fix "we
chose," a liked direction becomes a spec "we agreed," a fresh draft
becomes a settled artifact. No step lies; each rounds up half a rung,
and after three steps the owner faces "decided" work he never decided.

Every task and decision sits on exactly one rung or terminal state,
named in its status:

- L0 claimed — a reviewer, critic, or the assistant asserts it; the
  owner has not spoken. Items are born here.
- L1 issue confirmed — the owner agrees the problem is real. Nothing
  about the fix is agreed.
- L2 direction agreed — the owner picked the shape of the fix. The
  details are not written.
- L3 spec drafted — a written spec exists and is under review. It is a
  draft, whoever wrote it.
- L4 ready — the owner signed the spec. Only now may it be built or
  folded into settled design docs.
- Terminal: rejected (the owner ruled the claim invalid) or done. Both
  leave the ladder and move to the changelog with a closing note.

The rules:

1. A critic's finding is a claim: it is born at L0. The owner's verdict
   is collected finding by finding, in conversation, before anything
   else happens to it.
2. A rung changes only on the owner's words, and the status QUOTES
   them. A quote is checkable; a date alone is not evidence and never
   suffices. If the words support L1, the status says L1 — even when
   the fix seems obvious.
3. One utterance moves an item at most one rung, and never past the
   rung its words support. Accepting a problem is not accepting a fix;
   accepting a direction is not accepting a plan.
4. Artifacts are born DRAFT. Any catalog, diagram, spec, or prompt the
   assistant produces carries draft status until the owner has walked
   through it.
5. The decision log records only decisions the owner made. Recording an
   assumption, a reviewer's conclusion, or the assistant's own
   inference as "accepted" is a defect; withdraw it when found.
6. Inference is not a ruling. "The thing you approved requires X" may
   be true — X still waits for the owner's word, with the inference
   written as a note beside it.
7. A verdict on unreadable text is not a verdict. Findings put to the
   owner must be readable: the concrete story, what exists today, the
   options, their costs. Compressed labels manufacture fake agreement.
8. Who types the rung: the assistant may type L0 through L3, quote
   always attached. L4 is typed only on an owner utterance that names
   the item and the rung — that utterance is the signature — and the
   owner may always edit the file himself instead.
9. Statuses are audited, in both directions. A periodic sweep asks of
   every status: what act does this claim, and where is that act
   recorded? Stale-low (work done, rung not raised) is the same defect
   as inflation.
10. When the owner's words are ambiguous, the rung is lower — and the
    ambiguity is resolved by asking, not left standing.
